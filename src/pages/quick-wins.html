Smaller changes, each one on its own

The five prototypes combine several improvements into larger pieces of work. These are the smaller ones from the same review: 27 changes that each stand alone, so any of them can be picked up without committing to the rest.

Each one shows what mantracgroup.com/en-eg does today, recreated from what we saw on 1 October 2026, next to how it would work instead.

What we're proposing

A set of changes that can be made one at a time and in any order, without a redesign. Most are about getting facts and wording right. A few deal with things that are broken or out of date, and two concern Egypt's data protection rules, which are enforced from 1 November 2026.

Why

  • Some of the most visible problems on the site are a wrong word, a dead link or a figure that disagrees with itself. None of those needs a project to fix.
  • Several of these also appear inside a prototype, where they are one part of something larger. They are shown here separately so each can be judged on its own.

Current range first

Today

The excavator listing has 27 results. Next to the 320 GX and 336 GC sit the 315C L, 320C L, 345B L Series II UHD, 365C, 385C and "318D2 L (2017)", which are older models a buyer can no longer order new.

The change

List the current range by default and move older models to a short "Previous models: parts and service" list underneath, so the comparison is between machines Mantrac can actually supply.

Open questionQ: Which models does Mantrac consider current for Egypt? The proposed list uses the models in the prototype data; the order on the live page is shown as we found it.

Commas for thousands

Today

Product cards show "3.050 kg" and "2.710 mm", and "6.725 lb" in US units. To an English reader that is three kilograms. The request-a-quote page shows the same machine as "3050 kg".

The change

Format every number from one function that knows the language: 3,050 kg in English, and Arabic numbers on the Arabic site, in whichever digits Mantrac prefers.

BehaviourThe browser's own number formatting does the work, so commas, Arabic digits and unit conversions come from one place rather than being typed into each product (R50).

Open questionQ: Does Mantrac want Arabic-Indic digits (٣٬٠٥٠) or Western digits (3,050) on the Arabic site? The Arabic labels here are a draft for Mantrac's translators to check.

Also part of prototype 1.

A compare tray you can see

Today

Each card has a "Compare model" tick box. Ticking it changes nothing else on the page, so there is no way to see what has been picked or where to go next.

The change

A tray appears once something is ticked, with the chosen models, a count and a "Compare" button that only becomes active with two or more.

BehaviourUp to three models. On the live site the tray would stay at the bottom of the screen and follow the visitor between pages; the Compare button opens the comparison from prototype 1 (R51).

Also part of prototype 1.

Stock status that agrees

Today

The 302.7 CR card says "Stock on demand". Its product page says "In stock". A rental card says both "Availability Reserved" and "In stock". A buyer can't tell which to believe.

The change

Four stock words, taken from one field and shown identically wherever the machine appears: in stock at a named branch, on order with a lead time, reserved, sold.

Open questionQ: Where does stock status come from today, and could branch and lead time come from the same place? Branch and lead time here are sample data.

Add to quote, not add to cart

Today

Each product card has a cart icon titled "Add to cart". It doesn't add anything to a cart: it opens the request-a-quote page for that one product.

The change

Label it "Add to quote" and let it collect machines, so a contractor pricing three of them sends one request. If a basket is a step too far for now, the label alone is still worth changing.

BehaviourAdds to the same quote as the prototypes; watch the Quote count in the top bar. The quote itself is prototype 3 (R52).

Also part of prototype 1 and prototype 3.

Retire the app panel

Today

The Used, Rental and Promotions pages all promote the "Mantrac Used&Rental Equipment app". Both the App Store and Google Play links return "not found", and did so when we checked them twice.

The change

Take the panel down. In its place, point to the used and rental listings on the website, which is where the same stock would be found anyway.

Open questionQ: Has the app been retired, or moved to a new store listing? If it is coming back, the panel can return with working links.

A five-field contact form

Today

The contact form has 11 fields and 9 are required, including Salutation and both Phone and Mobile Phone. The message box is optional. Every field uses its placeholder as its label, which disappears as soon as someone starts typing, and Email is a plain text field.

The change

Five fields with visible labels: what you need, name, mobile, company and message. Job title and industry can be asked later, by the person who replies.

BehaviourTry sending it empty: each field says what is missing. On success the visitor gets a reference and is told who will reply and when. The reply time is sample (R54).

One contact bar on mobile

Today

On a 390px phone, four fixed widgets sit over the page: a contact rail of unlabelled icons, a 276 by 162 pixel chat invitation, a chat button and a feedback tab. The invitation covers the product cards. Around two thirds of web visits in Egypt are on mobile.

One labelled bar at the bottom of the screen: Call 19266, WhatsApp, Branch and Chat. Chat opens when someone asks for it, and feedback moves to the footer.

A hotline link that dials

Today

The hotline in the header links to tel:+2019266. Short-code hotlines in Egypt are normally dialled as the short code alone, so the country code may stop the call connecting from a mobile.

The change

Test the link on Egyptian networks, and link to tel:19266 if that is what works. Show a landline alongside it for anyone calling from abroad, who can't reach a short code at all.

Open questionQ: Has anyone at Mantrac tried the website's hotline link from an Egyptian mobile? And which landline should callers abroad use?

The change

Show the page, its menu link and the footer promise only while there is at least one live offer. When the last one ends, all three disappear on their own and come back with the next.

BehaviourThe offers are sample data from prototype 4, filtered to those running on 1 October 2026 (R57).

Real dates on news

Today

Every item on the first Media page is dated 09 June 2025, including a story about keeping a factory running during the pandemic and the Ever Given rescue from 2021. "Sort by: Most recent" can't do anything useful when every date is the same.

The change

Restore the original publication dates from the old site or the articles themselves, and hide the date where it genuinely can't be found rather than showing a wrong one.

Open questionQ: Can the original dates be recovered from the previous site? The Ever Given was freed in March 2021; the other dates are sample, and story titles are shortened or in brackets.

A proofreading pass

Today

"ciritical tests" on Parts, "ALL EQUIPMENTS" and "Your trusting partner" on Construction, "S.O.SSM" in search results, Media titles cut off mid-word, and "Copyright © 2025" in the footer.

The change

One spelling and style pass across the site, a copyright year that updates itself, and titles that wrap onto a second line instead of being cut.

Consent before tracking

Today

The homepage loads Google Ads, LinkedIn, Facebook, Eloqua, Pardot and Qualtrics as soon as it opens. We saw no cookie banner or consent tool. The executive regulations of Egypt's Personal Data Protection Law are enforced from 1 November 2026.

The change

A consent tool, in English and Arabic, that holds back everything non-essential until the visitor agrees, with a cookie policy behind it. Mantrac's own counsel should confirm exactly what the law requires.

BehaviourThe list shows which tags would load after each choice. "Cookie settings" reopens the banner, as a footer link would on the live site (R60).

Open questionQ: Where is Mantrac with the data protection law: a consent tool, a licence for electronic direct marketing, a data protection officer? This is a summary of published legal commentary, not legal advice. The Arabic is a draft.

A sign-up that asks permission

Today

The footer sign-up asks only for an email address, labelled "Comments" in the page's text, with nothing about what will be sent, no consent wording and no privacy link. Under the same law, electronic marketing needs prior consent.

The change

A labelled field, a sentence on what is sent and how often, an unticked consent box and a privacy link.

BehaviourThe consent box starts unticked and is required. On the live site the address would be confirmed by email before anything is sent (R61).

Also part of prototype 4.

Language tags that point somewhere

Today

Each English Egypt page tells search engines it has Russian and Chinese versions, whose addresses are "#". It doesn't declare itself as the English version or give a default. The Arabic tag is correct.

The change

Declare only the versions that exist (English and Arabic for Egypt) plus a default, on every page. Visitors never see this, but it affects which version search engines show to whom.

The Russian and Chinese language codes are shown in their simplest form; the addresses are as we found them.

Pictures when links are shared

Today

The homepage has no share image, so a link to it sent on WhatsApp or posted on LinkedIn arrives without a picture. For a business that invites enquiries by WhatsApp on every page, the preview is much of the first impression.

The change

A share image, title and description on every page template, using the machine photograph on product pages.

BehaviourThe preview text and image come from tags in each page; WhatsApp, LinkedIn and Facebook read the same ones (R62).
